Study Guide Method

How GTS students study

GTS has fully adopted the study methodology of GBU, which has developed a unique method of study.

Reflection & Discussion

This part of the course is designed to let the student interact with the mentor on a one-to-one basis. While there is much freedom to respond, all responses should ultimately end within the framework of a Christian worldview and be defended by the Word of God. If, upon discussion, the mentor finds that a student's answer needs to be more biblically based, he will have the student either rewrite or add to a portion of their response.

Scriptural Memory Work

The student is expected to memorise and recite, without help, the passages of Scripture indicated, in order to complete the individual course. Chapter and verse must be included in the memorisation response.

Personal Application

Practical suggestions are offered on how the material studied can be applied in life. Questions and answers are based upon the text — there will be no answer that cannot be easily found within the material. Each student is encouraged to use a complete sentence when responding, so as to learn to think clearly and logically.

Self-Assessment

This is a private and personal assessment of one's own heart. It does not need to be shared or discussed.

"Examine yourselves as to whether you are in the faith. Test yourselves. Do you not know yourselves, that Jesus Christ is in you — unless indeed you are disqualified?" — 2 Cor. 13:5

Course Descriptions

A sample of what you'll study

Biblical Studies

  • PBIB 101 · How to Study the Bible. A work by R. A. Torrey designed to help the student learn the major concepts and principles of Bible study. (3 semester units)
  • BIB 102 · Introduction to the Scriptures. Utilizes W. Graham Scroggie's classic Know Your Bible, providing a brief introduction to all the books of the Bible. (3 semester units)Know Your Bible
  • BIB 201 · Biblical Hermeneutics I. A study of the issues, history, and principles of biblical interpretation, using Dr. W. R. Downing's An Introduction to Biblical Hermeneutics, Part I. (3 semester units)An Introduction to Biblical Hermeneutics
  • BIB 202 · Biblical Hermeneutics II. Continues the study of interpretation using Part II of Downing's hermeneutics text. (3 semester units)

Survey & Languages

  • BIB 203 · Old Testament Survey I. Genesis through the Song of Solomon, with introduction, analysis, and practical observations for each book (Downing). (3 semester units)
  • BIB 204 · Old Testament Survey II. Isaiah through the intertestamental era, with introduction, analysis, and practical observations. (3 semester units)
  • BIB 205 · New Testament Survey I. Matthew through Acts. (3 semester units)
  • BIB 206 · New Testament Survey II. The life and epistles of the Apostle Paul. (3 semester units)
  • BIB 301 · New Testament Survey III. The General Epistles and Revelation. (3 semester units)
  • Elementary Greek & Elementary Hebrew. Foundational study of the biblical languages. (3 semester units each)

Revival & Missionary Studies

  • History of Revival. A study of God's reviving work in the church across the centuries.
  • The Life of George Müller. The testimony of a man of faith — prayer, providence, and practical trust in God.
  • New Testament Missionary. The pattern of missions in the New Testament church.

Exposition & Pastoral Theology

  • New Testament Exposition. The discipline of faithfully explaining and applying the Word of God.
  • Pastoral Theology. The work of the pastor — shepherding, teaching, and leading the local church.
  • Theology of Prayer. The biblical foundation and practice of prayer.
